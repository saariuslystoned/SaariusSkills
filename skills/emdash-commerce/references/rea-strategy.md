# Use REA for the current decision

Reverse-engineering analysis (REA) supplies evidence, not a feature backlog or
authority to change direction. Select it for an actual product, principle,
architecture, implementation, or verification question. No predefined workflow
or roadmap is required. Illustrative merchant pains, vendor catalogs, and counts
of tools or generated obligations do not become build assignments.

## Find the relevant evidence

Discover the authorized research corpus through current project instructions or
their explicit private binding. Do not assume a repository, path, or access
right. If no source is available, state the gap and use relevant official public
sources or propose a bounded investigation; do not invent findings or start a
new harvest without scope.

Read the relevant index or synthesis first, then follow citations into source
evidence for claims that affect the recommendation. Record the revision or
capture date and material limitations. A research backlog entry establishes
intent, not completed findings. Avoid loading the entire corpus.

Keep these evidence types distinct:

| Evidence | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Published schema or documentation | A documented interface or claim, subject to version and availability limits |
| Static client code or bundle | Shipped references and possible behavior; not successful execution |
| Recorded or replayed exchanges | The captured scenarios, not all paths or live behavior |
| Live observation | Behavior in the observed account, configuration and moment |
| Current DinkusKit proof | Behavior actually verified at the named revision and runtime |

Withheld writes and simulated environments do not prove completed live writes.
Unobserved behavior or an absent captured endpoint is not proof of unsupported
functionality. Identify stale sources, capture gaps, and contradictions; do not
equate vendor evidence with implemented DinkusKit support.

## Reconcile before recommending

Check the owning repositories' current charters, explicit owner decisions,
accepted designs, source and proof. Separate research, accepted design, active
work, deferrals and verified implementation. A newer explicit owner decision
can supersede an old synthesis; surface the conflict without silently rewriting
a lock. An unfinished grill remains unfinished.

Translate useful patterns rather than copying the vendor's product. The result
may be a design rule, test, recovery behavior, integration, documentation change,
or no change. Recommend **adopt, adapt, defer, reject, or investigate** with the
evidence, fit to existing contracts, and smallest meaningful proof or owner
decision needed. These are recommendations, not lifecycle states or permission
to implement.

Normally defer unrelated discoveries so research does not continually delay a
release. Exceptions are contextual: explain why the finding matters now, what
work would change or slip, and obtain any needed scope decision. Do not silently
reprioritize, or treat a fixed list of exceptions as automatic permission.

## Preserve unresolved questions in existing records

First search current instructions, decisions, source, issues, Discussions and
proposals. Distinguish a discoverable fact, an already-made decision, and an
unanswered product choice. Investigate facts before asking the owner; preserve
conflicting evidence when a choice still needs resolution.

Use one primary durable record for each question, following its owning
repository's process:

- A reproduced bug or actionable task fits an issue.
- An unsettled product or architecture choice fits an established Discussion,
  proposal, or needs-decision issue.
- A private or vague research lead stays in authorized private research until
  it supports a useful, public-safe question.
- A cross-plugin question has one coordinating record linked from affected
  repositories, rather than independent copies of the same decision.

Search and deduplicate before filing. Retain the question, affected domain and
repositories, why it matters, evidence and revision, constraints, supported
options, blocking status, and a revisit trigger. Link a resolution when settled.
Use the existing record's fields; do not introduce a second queue, required
schema, periodic scheduler, or an issue per generated obligation.

Filing or updating a forge record follows the destination's policies and the
task's existing authorization. Where human posting is required, prepare the
proposed text and destination; a local draft is not a published canonical record.

At the start or resumption of a relevant DinkusKit grill, query pertinent open
records by topic, domain and owning repository. Reassess stale questions and
surface only those relevant to that grill. Inaccessible records are a discovery
limitation, not evidence that everything is settled. A later question keeps a
revisit trigger, such as a relevant grill or completion of missing research.

If an unresolved choice blocks current work, ask one focused owner question and
pause only dependent work; continue independent authorized work. A supported
candidate can enter the existing GrillTrack process. If no candidate answer is
justified, keep the question in its existing record: do not fabricate a choice
to satisfy `propose --choice`. Use the supported CLI for actual ledger changes;
never hand-edit or duplicate its history.

## Keep private evidence private

Public artifacts should contain original requirements, reasoning and tests.
Keep private source trails in their authorized records. Do not publish private
paths, captures, merchant data or copied vendor code, and honor the corpus's
handling rules. Reading research grants no new access, publication or live-action
authority.
